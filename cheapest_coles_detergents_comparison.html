3. Deep Chemical Research: Why ALDI Trimat Wins Testing & How Budget Powders Behave

3.1 The Trimat Advanced Chemistry: How a $5.25/kg Powder Tied Omo Ultimate (82% CHOICE Score)

In Australian consumer testing by CHOICE, ALDI Trimat Advanced Concentrated Laundry Powder achieved an extraordinary equal-first rating of 82% for front-loading washing machines, matching Omo Ultimate (which costs over $14.00/kg). A chemical autopsy reveals why Trimat punches so dramatically above its price tag:

1. Full Quattro-Enzyme Biocatalysis:

• Most budget detergents cut costs by omitting enzymes completely (LaundRite, Coles Simply) or including only a single cheap protease (Fab).

• Trimat Advanced incorporates a four-tier enzyme system:
• Alkaline Subtilisin (Protease): Cleaves blood, egg, and sweat peptides.
• Alpha-Amylase: Liquefies sticky starch glues from pasta, sauces, and baby food.
• Lipase: Hydrolyzes hydrophobic triglycerides from cooking oils and cosmetic sebum.
• Cellulase: Shaves micro-pills on cotton fibers, preventing grey soil redeposition.

2. Activated Oxygen Bleach (Percarbonate + TAED):

• Standard oxygen bleach (Sodium Percarbonate) requires wash temperatures above 50 °C to rapidly generate perhydroxyl anions ($HO_2^-$). In typical Australian cold-water washes (20 °C), unactivated bleach is slow and sluggish.

• Trimat Advanced formulates 1.5–3.0% TAED alongside 10–14% Percarbonate. TAED undergoes rapid perhydrolysis at 20 °C, generating peracetic acid ($CH_3COOOH$). Peracetic acid is a vastly more aggressive oxidizer than hydrogen peroxide, obliterating tea, coffee, wine, and fruit stains in short cold wash cycles.

3.2 The Inert Bulking Trap: LaundRite ($1.12/kg) & Coles Simply ($1.25/kg)

How can ALDI sell a 4kg box of LaundRite for $4.49, and Coles sell Coles Simply for $5.00?

The 70% Non-Cleaning Salt Reality:

• In LaundRite and Coles Simply, Sodium Sulfate ($Na_2SO_4$) comprises 64% to 72% of the carton. In an average 100g scoop, you are tossing 70g of inert mineral salt into your washer and only ~5g of cleaning surfactant.

• In contrast, Trimat Advanced and Biozet Attack Regular compress filler down to 15–25%, replacing it with insoluble Zeolite A water softeners, concentrated surfactant complexes, and active oxygen bleaches.

Why LaundRite Succeeded in CHOICE Top-Loader Tests:

• In CHOICE top-loader testing, LaundRite scored a very respectable 69% (winning its budget top-loader category).

• Top loaders use enormous water volumes (~60–80L per cycle) compared to front loaders (~15–20L). High water volume completely dissolves high-sulfate salts and dilutes hardness. In top loaders washing lightly soiled clothes, LaundRite's high alkalinity (Soda Ash) saponifies surface sebum effectively for pennies per wash. However, for tough food stains or front loaders, its lack of enzymes and bleaches causes it to drop off severely.

3.3 The Sensitive Skin Battle: ALDI Trimat Sensitive vs. Coles Ultra Sensitive ($5.25/kg)

Both ALDI (Trimat Sensitive) and Coles (Coles Ultra Sensitive) price their sensitive 2kg concentrates identically at $10.49 – $10.50 ($5.25/kg). Both advertise: "Free from added dyes, perfumes, and enzymes."

Why Enzymes Are Omitted (Preventing Protease Keratolysis):

• Skin Barrier Protection: Protease (Subtilisin) cleaves peptide bonds in proteins. In babies and people suffering from eczema or damaged skin barriers, residual protease entrapped in clothing fibers can chemically digest the keratin in human stratum corneum, inducing burning, flaking, and severe contact dermatitis.

• Elimination of Respiratory Hazard (H334): Airborne enzyme dust is regulated by Safe Work Australia as H334: May cause allergy or asthma symptoms if inhaled. Both sensitive powders eliminate this hazard entirely.

How They Clean Without Enzymes:

Neither product reduces its active builder engine. Both retain:
• Zeolite A Ion Exchangers (18–24%): Traps hard water calcium without chalky precipitation.
• Alkaline Saponification (28–36% Soda Ash): Converts skin oils directly into soluble soaps.
• Hypoallergenic Oxygen Bleach (8–12%): Sanitizes and lifts stains cleanly without chlorine fumes or perfume masking agents.

3.4 Residue Forensic: Do Any ALDI or Coles Powders Leave Fabric Residues?

A common consumer concern with laundry powders is fabric residue, towel stiffness, and skin scratching. Official manufacturer data confirms:

1. Cationic Softener Residue (Zero in Standard Powders):

• Zero Waxy Cationic Softeners: None of the standard supermarket powders from ALDI, Coles, Henkel (Cold Power / Fab), or Biozet Regular contain cationic quaternary ammonium softening agents (e.g., dialkyl esterquats). Cationic quats are chemically incompatible with anionic powder surfactants (LAS) — they instantly precipitate into an insoluble gummy sludge. Towel absorbency remains 100% unimpaired.

• The Bentonite Clay Mechanism: According to official disclosures from Kao Australia (Biozet Attack PLUS Softener) and Henkel Australia (Cold Power 2 in 1 with a Touch of Fabric Softener), powders advertising built-in softening exclusively use natural mineral Bentonite Clay (Bentonite Clay - Natural Softening Agent). Bentonite softens mechanically by depositing microscopic flat aluminosilicate platelets between cotton fibers to lubricate them. However, continuous use in short, cold wash cycles can cause fine insoluble mineral residue accumulation over time.

2. Mineral Residue (Zeolite Dust vs. Carbonate Scale):

• Zeolite A White Streaking: ALDI Trimat, ALDI Almat Conc, Coles Ultra, Cold Power, and Biozet use Zeolite A (Sodium Aluminosilicate). Zeolites are insoluble microscopic mineral cages (~2–4 microns). In short cold cycles with overloaded drums, undissolved zeolite particles can lodge in the weave of dark synthetic fabrics, appearing as faint white streaks. A standard rinse cycle clears this completely.

• Calcium Carbonate Chalking: In contrast, budget powders (LaundRite, Coles Simply) lack zeolites. In hard water, their high soda ash causes calcium to precipitate as crystalline chalk (CaCO₃) directly inside towel fibers, producing extreme scratchiness and fabric stiffness over time.

5. Victorian Water Quality & Machine Dosing Advice

  • Greater Melbourne (Yarra Ranges Catchment, Soft Water 10–25 mg/L CaCO₃): Melbourne has some of the softest municipal water in the world. This soft water allows ALDI Trimat Advanced and Coles Ultra to achieve peak detergency at just 20g to 25g per wash (roughly half a scoop). In soft water, reducing powder dosage avoids zeolite micro-dust deposition and prevents surfactant overdosing.
  • Regional Victoria (Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, Hard Water 60–150 mg/L CaCO₃): Calcium and magnesium minerals in regional Victorian groundwater rapidly neutralize budget powders like LaundRite and Coles Simply because they lack zeolites. In regional Victoria, always select a zeolite-rich formulation like ALDI Trimat Advanced, Coles Ultra Concentrate, or Biozet Attack Regular to prevent rough chalk scale on towels.
  • Dissolution Advice for Melbourne Winters: In winter, tap water in Victoria can drop to 12–14 °C. Compact powders containing Zeolite A should be dosed directly into the machine drum rather than the dispenser drawer to avoid cold-water caking in the dispenser chute.
