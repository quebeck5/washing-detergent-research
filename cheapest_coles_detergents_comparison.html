Executive Summary: The Chemical Reality of Ultra-Budget Detergents

Across the Coles laundry aisle in Victoria, unit prices range from $1.00/L (Coles Simply) up to $14.50/L (Dynamo 7-in-1), and from $1.25/kg (Coles Simply 4kg) up to $11.00/kg (Radiant / Biozet). What chemical compromises enable these 5 products to sell at such ultra-low price points?

1. The "Filler vs. Active" Divide: In Coles Simply Powder ($1.25/kg), nearly 70% of the carton is inert Sodium Sulfate filler. A full 100g scoop delivers only ~5g of actual cleaning surfactant. By contrast, concentrated powders (Coles Ultra and Biozet) use active zeolite water-softeners and double the surfactant concentration.

2. The Omission of Biocatalytic Enzymes: High-purity enzymes (Protease, Amylase, Cellulase) represent up to 25% of the raw material cost in premium detergents. Both Coles Simply Liquid, Coles Simply Powder, and Earth Choice Standard completely omit enzymes. While they can wash away surface dust and loose sweat, they cannot chemically digest set-in proteins (blood, egg, grass) or starches (gravy, pasta) in cold water.

3. The "Water Truck" Effect: Coles Simply Liquid ($1.00/L) is ~90% purified water, thickened mechanically with common table salt (NaCl) via micellar entanglement rather than expensive polymeric rheology modifiers.

3. Deep Chemical Research: How Budget Detergent Chemistry Works & Its Limitations

3.1 The Economics of Bulking: Sodium Sulfate Fillers vs. Active Chemistry

How can Coles Simply Laundry Powder sell 4kg for just $5.00 ($1.25/kg)?

Sodium Sulfate (Glauber's Salt, Na₂SO₄):

Sodium sulfate is a neutral mineral salt costing less than $100 per tonne at industrial scale. In Coles Simply Powder, it accounts for 65% to 72% of total box weight.

The Physical Mechanism: It does not clean. Its chemical function is purely physical: it coats surfactant droplets during spray-drying to create free-flowing granules that do not clump in high humidity.

The Consumer Impact: In a standard 100g dosage scoop of Coles Simply, the consumer is actually dispensing 70g of inert mineral salt and only ~6g of surfactant. In contrast, a 50g scoop of concentrated powder (Biozet / Coles Ultra) delivers ~12g of active surfactant and ~10g of ion-exchange zeolites.

Greywater & Garden Hazard of Budget Powders:

Because Coles Simply Powder contains massive quantities of sodium sulfate and sodium carbonate, the resulting wash water has an exceptionally high Sodium Adsorption Ratio (SAR).

Effect on Soil: When used as garden greywater, sodium ions displace calcium and magnesium on clay soil particles, breaking down soil structure, sealing pores, and causing waterlogging and leaf scorch on native Australian plants. Coles Simply Powder should never be used on lawns or gardens.

3.2 The Enzyme Absence Penalty: Why Ultra-Cheap Detergents Fail on Food & Biological Stains

Coles Simply Liquid, Coles Simply Powder, and Earth Choice Standard contain 0% enzymes.

The Biocatalytic Cleavage Gap:

Enzymes are expensive biological catalysts produced via industrial microbial fermentation. A single enzyme molecule can cleave thousands of substrate bonds per minute in cold water:

  • Protease: Hydrolyzes peptide bonds in blood, egg, dairy, meat gravy, and sweat keratin.
  • Amylase: Liquefies sticky starch polymers (rice, pasta, potato, barbecue sauce).

Without enzymes, a detergent must rely solely on surface tension reduction and mechanical machine agitation. In cold water (20 °C), surfactants alone cannot solubilize cross-linked proteins or gelatinized starches. Stains set permanently into the fabric fibers.

Why Coles Ultra Infusions Outperforms Simply:

At $3.00/kg (or $3.00/L), Coles Ultra Infusions crosses the threshold into enzymatic cleaning:

  • It incorporates Subtilisin Protease and Amylase enzymes.
  • It adds Sodium Percarbonate (in the powder) to generate in situ hydrogen peroxide for tea, coffee, and wine bleaching.

This is why Coles Ultra routinely scores in the 65–70% range in Choice testing, while entry-level budget powders score in the 45–52% range (barely better than washing with plain tap water!).

3.3 Plant-Based Glucosides vs. Petrochemical LAS/SLES (The Earth Choice Trade-Off)

Earth Choice ($3.30/L) represents an entirely different formulation philosophy from the other four budget detergents:

Renewable Alkyl Polyglucosides (Coco-Glucoside):

Derived from coconut oil fatty alcohols and corn starch glucose. Non-ionic, non-ethoxylated, and completely petroleum-free.

  • Zero 1,4-Dioxane: Unlike SLES, it requires no ethoxylation, avoiding trace carcinogenic byproduct concerns.
  • Ultra-Low Aquatic Toxicity: Rapidly and completely mineralizes to CO₂ and H₂O in river systems (OECD 301D compliant).
  • Dermal Mildness: Extremely gentle on the skin mantle, making it ideal for eczema sufferers and baby clothing.
The Detersive Trade-Off:

Alkyl polyglucosides have larger polar headgroups with lower packing parameters than linear alkylbenzene sulfonates (LAS).

  • Particulate Soil Suspension: They are significantly less effective at suspending heavy red clay, mud, or motor oil compared to synthetic LAS.
  • Water Hardness Sensitivity: In hard water areas of regional Victoria, plant soaps precipitate unless heavily buffered with sodium citrate.

3.4 The Salt Thickening Trick: How $1.00/L Liquid Fools the Eye

When pouring Coles Simply Laundry Liquid ($1.00/L), consumers observe a rich, honey-like viscosity that mimics premium $13.00 liquids. How is this achieved in a formula that is 90% water with only ~6% surfactant?

The "Salt Curve" Mechanism: Formulators add 1% to 2% ordinary table salt (Sodium Chloride, NaCl). The addition of Cl⁻ and Na⁺ ions screens the electrostatic repulsion between the negatively charged sulfate headgroups of SLES molecules. This causes spherical micelles to elongate into rod-like, entangled worm-like micelles that physically trap water, artificially multiplying viscosity without adding costly cleaning polymers.

Why does Coles stock a $1.00/L detergent on the same aisle as a $14.50/L detergent?

The Coles Private Label Ladder: Defending Against Aldi While Driving Margin

In Australia, Aldi aggressively targets price-sensitive shoppers with its ultra-cheap Almat and Tandil private labels. Coles uses Coles Simply as a "price fighter" barrier: it guarantees that any shopper walking into Coles sees a detergent priced at or below Aldi's entry price point, stopping customers from walking out to discount grocers.

5. Victorian Water Conditions & Dosing Guidance

How to get the best performance from these ultra-budget detergents under Victorian wash conditions:

  • Melbourne Soft Water Synergy: Greater Melbourne’s tap water (10–25 mg/L CaCO₃) allows budget detergents like Coles Simply Liquid to work significantly better than they would in hard-water cities (Adelaide/Brisbane). Without calcium ions interfering, low surfactant concentrations (5–8%) are sufficient to clean lightly soiled clothes.
  • Regional Victorian Hard Water Caution: If washing in Geelong, Ballarat, or Bendigo with hard water (60–150 mg/L CaCO₃), Coles Simply Powder will struggle because it lacks zeolite water softeners. The free calcium precipitates the low surfactant load into insoluble scum. In regional Victoria, step up to Coles Ultra Powder (which contains Zeolite A) or add half a scoop of laundry booster.
  • Food Stain Remediation: If using Coles Simply or Earth Choice, set-in protein stains (blood, dairy, grass, collar grease) must be pre-treated with a dedicated enzyme stain-remover spray (e.g. Sard Wonder or Coles Ultra Oxy spray), as the wash liquor contains zero enzymes.
  • Septic System Warning: Never use Coles Simply Powder in homes connected to aerated septic tanks or absorption trenches due to high sodium sulfate salinity. Use Earth Choice or Coles Simply Liquid instead.
