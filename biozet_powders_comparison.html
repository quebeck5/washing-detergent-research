Executive Summary: Powder Architecture Comparison

Across Victoria, Biozet Attack markets exactly three washing machine powder variants (available in 1kg, 2kg, 5.4kg, and 6kg carton packs). While they share a high-performance alkaline builder chassis (sodium carbonate, zeolite, and sodium silicate), their functional active chemistry is fundamentally tailored:

1. Regular Powder: A balanced stain-removal formula combining Sodium Percarbonate (oxygen bleach), Multi-Enzymes (protease/amylase), and Optical Brighteners for maximum everyday whitening.

2. PLUS Softener Powder: Removes oxygen bleach completely to preserve fabric conditioners, replacing it with Bentonite Clay (micro-platelet lubricating softening agent) while retaining the multi-enzyme cleaning engine and optical brighteners.

3. PLUS Eliminator Powder: A dedicated biocide and odour oxidation powerhouse. It pairs Sodium Percarbonate with DOBS (Sodium dodecanoyloxybenzene sulfonate) to generate cold-water active perlauric acid. To prevent chemical conflicts and fabric discolouration on sports synthetics, all enzymes and optical brighteners are intentionally omitted.

3. In-Depth Chemical Research: How the Differences in Formulation Work & Their Direct Effects

A detailed scientific breakdown of the molecular reactions, physical mechanics, and comparative effects across the three Biozet Attack washing machine powders.

3.1 Oxygen Bleaching & Cold-Water Antimicrobial Activation (DOBS vs. Percarbonate vs. Bleach-Free)

The single largest chemical divide across the powders is the bleaching and sanitation system:

Standard Oxygen Bleach (Regular Powder)

Active Agent: Sodium Percarbonate (2Na₂CO₃·3H₂O₂).
Dissolution Reaction:
2Na₂CO₃·3H₂O₂ (s) → 2Na₂CO₃ (aq) + 3H₂O₂ (aq) Mechanism & Limitation: Dissolves to produce free hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂). Under alkaline conditions (pH ~10.5), H₂O₂ partially dissociates into the perhydroxyl anion (HOO⁻), which cleaves conjugated chromophore double bonds in wine, coffee, and fruit stains. However, at cold water temperatures (< 40 °C), H₂O₂ has a high kinetic activation barrier; its bleaching rate is moderate unless wash time is prolonged or water temperature is raised.

Activated Perlauric Acid System (PLUS Eliminator)

Active Agents: Sodium Percarbonate + Sodium Dodecanoyloxybenzene Sulfonate (DOBS).
In Situ Perhydrolysis Reaction:
C₁₁H₂₃-COO-C₆H₄-SO₃Na + HOO⁻ → C₁₁H₂₃-COOOH (Perlauric Acid) + ⁻O-C₆H₄-SO₃Na Mechanism & Distinct Effect: DOBS is an acyloxybenzene sulfonate that reacts rapidly with HOO⁻ even at 20 °C to generate perlauric acid (peroxydodecanoic acid):

  • Lipophilic Membrane Penetration: Unlike standard hydrophilic H₂O₂ or European TAED (which produces water-soluble peracetic acid), perlauric acid carries a long C₁₁ aliphatic hydrophobic tail. This gives it amphiphilic surfactant properties that penetrate lipid biofilms and bacterial membranes (e.g. Moraxella osloensis), destroying bacterial cells for 99.9% odour elimination.
  • Cold Water Efficacy: Delivers high oxidation power in cold Victorian tap water without requiring warm wash cycles.

Bleach-Free Formulation (PLUS Softener)

Bleach Status: 0% Sodium Percarbonate.
Chemical Rationale: The absence of oxidative bleaching agents protects delicate fiber finishes, prevents the oxidation of smectite clay conditioning agents, preserves floral rose fragrance notes, and eliminates the risk of colour fading on sensitive dyes.

3.2 The Physics & Chemistry of Bentonite Clay Softening vs. Liquid Quats

Biozet Attack PLUS Softener uses natural mineral Bentonite Clay (CAS 1302-78-9) rather than synthetic quaternary ammonium compounds:

Bentonite Smectite Platelet Interleaving

Mineral Structure: Bentonite is an expandable 2:1 phyllosilicate clay consisting of an octahedral alumina sheet sandwiched between two tetrahedral silica sheets.

Mechanism of Action: In the wash bath, water molecules penetrate the clay's interlayer galleries, causing the particles to delaminate into microscopic, flat, flexible platelets (~1 nm thick and ~200 nm wide). Under mechanical agitation, these negatively charged mineral platelets electrostatically deposit between the cellulose fibrils of cotton yarns.

Anti-Stiffness Effect: When line-dried, cotton fibrils normally form rigid inter-fiber hydrogen bonds, creating a stiff, scratchy texture. The intercalated bentonite platelets act as a microscopic dry solid lubricant (similar to microscopic ball bearings), allowing fibers to bend and slide smoothly over one another.

3.3 Biocatalytic Cleavage: The Multi-Enzyme Cocktail & Why Eliminator Omits It

Both Biozet Attack Regular and PLUS Softener powders incorporate a high-potency enzyme engine. Here is how each enzyme functions:

Alkaline Protease (Subtilisin)

Target: Insoluble protein soils (blood, egg, dairy, grass, collar sweat, skin keratin).
Mechanism: Employs a catalytic triad (Asp32-His64-Ser221) to hydrolyze internal peptide bonds (-CO-NH-), cutting long protein polymers into small, water-soluble peptides and amino acids that are lifted away by surfactants.

Alpha-Amylase

Target: Starch-based food residues (pasta, rice, potato, gravies, baby food, chocolate).
Mechanism: Endohydrolyzes α-(1,4)-D-glucosidic bonds in amylose and amylopectin. Starch normally acts as a sticky biological binder holding particulate dirt to cotton fibers; amylase liquefies the starch glue, releasing trapped soils.

Care Cellulase

Target: Microfibril fuzz and cotton fabric pilling.
Mechanism: Selectively cleaves amorphous, disordered cellulose chains protruding from damaged cotton fibers. By shaving off microscopic microfibrillar fuzz, it releases trapped particulate soot and restores smooth fabric reflectance, eliminating the "gray veil".

Why PLUS Eliminator Completely Omits Enzymes: The in situ generation of perlauric acid creates an intensely oxidative environment. Peracids rapidly oxidize the critical methionine (Met) and tryptophan (Trp) amino acid residues adjacent to the active catalytic pocket of enzymes, completely denaturing them within seconds. Biozet formulated Eliminator around chemical peracid oxidation rather than enzymatic biocatalysis.

3.4 Photophysical Whitening: Disodium CBS-X vs. Brightener-Free Formulations

Photophysics of CBS-X (In Regular & Softener)

Chemical Agent: Disodium Distyrylbiphenyl Disulfonate (Tinopal CBS-X, CAS 27344-41-8).
Mechanism: CBS-X is a planar aromatic stilbene with high water solubility. It absorbs invisible ultraviolet radiation from sunlight (wavelength λ ≈ 340–360 nm) and re-emits this energy as visible blue fluorescence (λ ≈ 430–445 nm) via a rapid radiative electronic transition (Stokes shift).

Visual Effect: Natural cotton fibers naturally absorb light in the blue region as they age, causing them to reflect a yellowish tint. The blue light emitted by CBS-X adds to the reflected light; through additive complementary color mixing (Yellow + Blue = White), white clothes appear visibly brighter and cleaner than untreated cotton.

Why PLUS Eliminator Is 100% Brightener-Free

Biozet Attack PLUS Eliminator specifically omits all optical brighteners for three critical functional reasons:

  • Sports Apparel & Synthetics: Technical athletic fabrics (polyester, nylon, elastane) lack the cellulose binding sites for CBS-X. Brighteners deposit unevenly on synthetics, causing blotchiness or yellow-green haze under bright sunlight.
  • Dark & Vibrant Garments: Optical brighteners deposit a permanent blue sheen onto dark, navy, and black garments, giving them a faded, chalky appearance.
  • Military, Law Enforcement & Hunting: Brightener-treated fabrics glow brilliantly under night-vision and UV illumination; brightener-free detergents are required for tactical and hunting apparel.

3.5 The Inorganic Builder Chassis: Saponification, Water Softening & Drum Protection

Sodium Aluminosilicate (Zeolite A)

Mechanism: A microporous hydrated aluminosilicate cage (pore size ~4.2 Å). It selectively captures water hardness ions (Ca²⁺ & Mg²⁺) through ion exchange, trading two non-interfering Na⁺ ions for every Ca²⁺ trapped. This prevents surfactants from precipitating as insoluble lime scum. Being zero-phosphorus (<0.5%), it avoids algal blooms in Victorian catchments.

Sodium Carbonate (Soda Ash)

Mechanism: Buffers the wash liquor to pH 10.2–11.0. This high alkaline pH hydrolyzes fatty triglycerides (sebum, body oils) into soluble sodium soaps (saponification) and establishes a negative zeta potential on both fabric and soil particles, creating mutual electrostatic repulsion that prevents dirt re-adsorption.

Sodium Silicate

Mechanism: Provides robust alkaline buffering and, crucially, deposits a nanometer-thin passivating polysilicate film over aluminum and steel components inside the washing machine. This protects internal pump impellers, heating elements, and stainless steel drums from corrosion caused by alkaline salts.

5. Commercial Strategy & Consumer Psychology: Why 3 Formulations at the Same Price?

In Australian supermarkets (Coles, Woolworths, IGA), Biozet Attack Regular, PLUS Softener, and PLUS Eliminator are sold at identical shelf prices (e.g. standard RRP ~$26.00 for 2kg, frequently promoted at ~$13.00). From a manufacturing and supply chain perspective, running three separate chemical formulas and SKUs incurs substantial operational overhead. Why does Kao do this?

The FMCG Supermarket Battle: "Shelf Blocking" & Category Domination

In Australian grocery retailing, shelf space is finite. If a brand offers only one SKU, Coles and Woolworths will allocate only 1 to 2 "facings" (product slots), filling the rest with rival brands like Omo, Cold Power, Radiant, and private-label generics. By fielding three targeted variants (Regular, Softener, Eliminator) across multiple pack sizes (1kg, 2kg, 5.4kg, 6kg), Kao captures a dominant visual billboard on the laundry aisle, effectively squeezing out competitor visibility.

How Kao Balances Formulation Costs Behind the Scenes:

To maintain equal retail margins across the three variants at the same retail price, Kao uses compensatory formulation engineering:

  • In PLUS Eliminator, the inclusion of the premium, proprietary bleach activator DOBS is commercially offset by eliminating the multi-enzyme cocktail and optical brighteners (both of which are high-cost specialty ingredients).
  • In PLUS Softener, abundant natural Bentonite clay is cost-effective, while the complete omission of Sodium Percarbonate oxygen bleach frees up budget for higher-tier rose perfumes.
  • In Regular, standard percarbonate and enzyme costs are balanced across massive production volumes, serving as the cost anchor for the entire lineup.

4. Victorian Water Quality & Practical Laundering Notes

Specific considerations for laundering with Biozet Attack powders in Victoria:

  • Melbourne Soft Water Advantage: Greater Melbourne municipal tap water (supplied by Melbourne Water from the Yarra Ranges) is exceptionally soft, typically measuring between 10 to 25 mg/L CaCO₃. Because Biozet powders contain high zeolite and carbonate builder levels, Melbourne users can safely use the lower recommended dosage scoop without experiencing soil redeposition or soap scum.
  • Regional Victorian Hard Water Areas: In parts of regional Victoria (such as Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, or the Mallee where bore or ground water is blended), water hardness can rise to 60–150 mg/L. In these areas, using the full scoop ensures the zeolite builders fully bind free Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ ions.
  • Cold Water Optimum: Biozet Attack powders are chemically engineered to achieve complete particle dissolution at 20 °C. To prevent dispenser gumming, ensure the powder drawer is completely dry before dosing, or add the powder directly to the empty drum before loading clothes.
  • Strict Prohibition on Chlorine Bleach: Never mix Biozet Attack powders with sodium hypochlorite (chlorine bleach), as violent decomposition, oxygen degassing, and neutralization of the detergent actives will occur.
